# Guided business studio

The applicant workspace now starts with saved ideas and a separate New idea area. Format cards offer a guided interview, free text, PDF and audio. Activity cards count only the latest twenty submissions; they are not application-wide totals. Assessment, Business plan, Explore market, Next steps and Versions & privacy keep the tools manageable. Hidden panels stay mounted so changing work areas does not discard answers or scratchpad inputs. Actor changes unmount the private workspace.

`GuidedIdea.tsx` asks twelve optional questions in three stages: idea, market and numbers. It composes labelled text exclusively from supplied answers and previews the exact source before submission. No financial defaults, invented customers or generated demand evidence are added. Explicit unknown answers remain in the original; blank answers are omitted. The normal authenticated submission, consent, synthetic marking, idempotency and durable worker contracts are reused. Draft answers stay in React memory, not local/session storage. Refreshing the page discards an unsubmitted draft.

Once an assessment exists, the API selects three unresolved fields. Each includes a reason, answer guidance and saved source references. Ambiguities precede missing details; source notes and original excerpts help the applicant identify the conflict. Stable question IDs and prepared localized questions remain intact. New contextual guidance is English and labelled as such alongside non-English questions. Human translation review is still pending. Trusted reviewer exclusions are skipped consistently in questions, answer validation and rule-based explanations.

There is no universal verified business benchmark in this project. The eighteen fields and six assessment dimensions are a proposed project rubric. Missing information is a reason to ask, not permission to impute an industry average. Applicants cannot exclude fields to increase their completeness score. Trusted reviewer exclusions retain their reason and denominator treatment.

`BusinessBlueprint.tsx` groups the saved facts into six business-map cards. Unknowns and conflicts stay visible. Its Markdown brief includes assessment version/date, self-reported status, unresolved details and original evidence, with no authorization token or scratchpad assumptions. It downloads to the user's device; no external document service is involved.

`ScenarioPlanner.tsx` and `domain/scenario.ts` provide a separate INR scratchpad. Five explicit operating assumptions produce monthly revenue, operating cost, contribution, break-even volume and operating surplus. Optional startup cost enables simple payback only when surplus is positive. Negative or zero unit contribution produces no break-even claim. A twenty-percent lower-sales scenario reduces volume and variable costs while keeping fixed costs constant. A pinned scenario compares monthly surplus. Blank, invalid, negative, infinite and impossible calendar inputs do not produce results.

This is illustrative arithmetic, not demand prediction, sector validation, a tax calculation or a loan schedule. Derived results that exceed safe paise precision, nonfinite payback or unsafe break-even counts are rejected. The scratchpad neither calls an assessment mutation API nor changes stored scores, facts or Groq explanations. Its assumptions and pinned values disappear on refresh, sign-out or selecting another idea. An eligible saved INR per-unit price can be copied only through an explicit button; all other assumptions start blank.

The studio uses the existing React/Lucide stack and shared theme variables, with no new runtime dependency or paid service. `studio.css` supplies responsive cards, segmented tool navigation, input stages, chart bars, mobile layouts and reduced-motion handling. Browser checks use actual local API/PostgreSQL with explicitly synthetic Auth/model/vector fixtures; they are not evidence of live model accuracy or public deployment. Existing measured inference, Groq, translation and source-quality limitations still apply.

For a committee demonstration: open an incomplete saved idea, show the proposed rubric and missing facts, answer a contextual follow-up, inspect the immutable version history, then use the business map and scratchpad to explain a concrete cost assumption. Show that the saved assessment is unchanged after stress testing. Use `input/` rehearsal files with their synthetic label.
